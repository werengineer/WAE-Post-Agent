# Engineering Employability in 2026: What the Numbers Say About Your Branch

The India Skills Report 2026 has one headline for engineers: employability for BE/BTech graduates is about 70%, a small slip from about 71.5% the year before (as reported by Careers360). That average hides a lot. Where you land depends heavily on your branch.

## The branch gap
- Computer Science: about 80%
- Information Technology: about 78%
- Instrumentation: about 77%
- Electronics & Communication: about 75%
- Mechanical: about 63%

A 17-point gap between Computer Science and Mechanical is large. Branch isn't destiny, though. Employability scores measure demonstrated skills on an assessment, and skills can be built.

## What the score actually measures
It is a test of job-readiness: applied problem solving, communication, and role-specific skill. It is not a measure of your CGPA. Two students with the same marks can score very differently.

## What to do about it
1. **Pick one role, not "any job."** Core, software and data roles ask for different things.
2. **Build one visible thing a semester.** A working circuit, a CAD model with a simulation, a deployed app. Proof beats claims.
3. **Practise explaining.** Walk a friend through your project in three minutes. Communication is part of the score.
4. **Learn the tools your branch uses.** Mechanical students: pair CAD with a simulation or Python for data. Electronics students: pair hardware with embedded code.

## A caution on the data
These figures come from news summaries of one report with its own method. Treat them as a direction, not a verdict, and compare year to year within the same report rather than across different studies.
