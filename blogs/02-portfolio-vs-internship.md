# Portfolio or Internship? The Order That Works

Entry-level tech hiring has been slow. A CodePath survey of 200+ engineering leaders, reported by Fortune in December 2025, points to portfolios and internships as the strongest readiness signals. NACE data cited in engineering-education research says more than 70% of employers treat internship experience as critical.

So which do you build first? Most advice we found says it isn't either/or. The sequence that holds up: **portfolio, then internship, then a stronger portfolio.**

## Step 1: A small portfolio gets you the interview
Internships are competitive and often start with a screening. Two or three finished projects, each with a short write-up, give a recruiter something to talk about. Finished beats ambitious.

## Step 2: The internship adds what projects can't
Working with a team, deadlines, code review or design review, and real constraints. Employers value this, which is why the NACE number is so high.

## Step 3: Feed the internship back into the portfolio
Write up what you learned (without sharing confidential material). Your next project will look more professional.

## What a good project write-up has
- The problem in one sentence
- What you built and what you chose not to build
- One thing that failed and what you changed
- A link: repo, demo, or photos/video

## No internship yet?
Put Projects right after Education and Skills on your resume. Contribute to an open-source project or a community build. Short, real work counts.

*Sources: Fortune/CodePath (Dec 2025); NACE via ASEE paper. We didn't use unverified "X% of hiring managers" claims found online. Verify before publishing.*
