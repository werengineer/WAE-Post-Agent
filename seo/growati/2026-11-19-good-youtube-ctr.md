# What Is a Good CTR on YouTube?

## The short answer

There is no single good number. YouTube's own Help Center says that half of all channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate (CTR) between 2% and 10%. New channels and videos less than a week old can show a wider range. The best comparison is your own channel's average, not a number from another channel.

Impressions CTR is how often people watched a video after seeing a registered impression on YouTube.

## Where to find your CTR in YouTube Studio

1. Sign in to YouTube Studio.
2. In the left menu, open **Analytics**.
3. For your whole channel, open the **Content** tab and look at **Impressions click-through rate**.
4. For one video, open that video's analytics and use its **Reach** tab.

YouTube's Help pages describe the metric as "Thumbnail impressions click-through rate": how often viewers watched a video after seeing a thumbnail. Studio's layout changes from time to time, so if a tab has moved, search YouTube Help for "impressions click-through rate".

Note: the key metrics card updates more often than the "Impressions and how they led to watch time" report, so the two numbers can differ for a short time.

## What counts as an impression (and what does not)

Not every time a thumbnail is on screen counts. YouTube says a thumbnail counts as an impression only if it is on screen for more than one second and at least half of it is visible. Impressions are not counted on external websites and apps, the YouTube Kids and YouTube Music apps, the mobile website, background tabs, or TrueView discovery ads.

So CTR uses only views that came from counted impressions. If you divide your total views by impressions, you will get a different number from the one Studio shows. That is expected.

## Why a "good" CTR depends on where people see your video

YouTube explains that placement changes CTR:

- A video shown to a very large audience, for example on the Home page, usually has a lower CTR.
- A video whose impressions mostly come from a channel page can show a higher CTR.
- Smaller videos often have a higher CTR because they reach a narrower, more loyal audience.

YouTube also notes that traffic source can explain CTR differences more than the title or thumbnail does. Before you decide a thumbnail failed, check the traffic source.

A high CTR is not always good. YouTube warns that clickbait can raise CTR while lowering average view duration, which can reduce recommendations and impressions.

## How long to wait before you judge a CTR

YouTube advises you to look at CTR after a substantial number of impressions, and not right after you upload. Videos with fewer than 100 views can see an even wider range. Small swings are normal. Act on a change only when you have enough data to trust it.

## What to do when one video's CTR is low

1. Check where the impressions come from before you blame the thumbnail.
2. Compare the video with your own recent videos on the same traffic source.
3. If the title and thumbnail really do not match what the video delivers, change one of them, not both, so you can tell what helped.
4. Write down the old title and thumbnail first so you can go back. Growati keeps version history and lets you restore an earlier version (stated on growati.com).
5. Wait for enough impressions, then compare again.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good CTR on YouTube?
YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. New channels and videos under a week old can vary more. Compare against your own channel average and the same traffic source, not against other channels.

### Where do I find CTR in YouTube Studio?
Open YouTube Studio, then Analytics. Use the Content tab for channel-wide impressions click-through rate. For one video, open that video's analytics and use the Reach tab. Studio's layout can change, so check YouTube Help if a tab has moved.

### What does impressions click-through rate mean?
It is how often viewers watched a video after seeing a registered impression of its thumbnail on YouTube. It only counts views that came from counted impressions.

### Why does my CTR not equal views divided by impressions?
Studio's CTR is based only on views from counted impressions. Some views come from places where impressions are not counted, such as external websites. So views divided by impressions can give a different figure.

### Why did my CTR drop when my views went up?
Wider exposure, for example on the Home page, usually lowers CTR, because more people who are less likely to click see the thumbnail. YouTube says traffic source can explain CTR differences more than the title or thumbnail does.

### How long should I wait before judging my CTR?
Wait until the video has a substantial number of impressions and do not check right after upload. YouTube says videos with fewer than 100 views can see an even wider range.

### Is a higher CTR always better?
No. YouTube warns that clickbait can raise CTR while lowering average view duration, which can reduce recommendations and impressions.
