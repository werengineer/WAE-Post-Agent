# Is My YouTube Channel Shadowbanned? How to Check

## In short

Probably not. The word "shadowban" means a secret block that YouTube never tells you about. We did not find a YouTube Help page that describes one. What YouTube does document are restrictions you can see in YouTube Studio, and normal reasons why impressions go down. Check those first, in this order: video notices, feature access, traffic sources, then packaging on your recent videos.

## What people mean by "shadowban"

Creators use the word for one situation: views or impressions fall, nothing explains it, and no email arrives. The fear is that YouTube is hiding your videos on purpose.

No one outside YouTube can prove that from the outside, and most third-party "shadowban checkers" are only guessing. So this guide does something different. It checks the things YouTube shows you, and the things YouTube says can lower your numbers. If both come back clean, the cause is almost always something you can change.

## Step 1: Look for restrictions you can actually see

YouTube Help says restrictions on your content show up in YouTube Studio:

1. Open YouTube Studio.
2. Select **Content** from the left menu.
3. Look at the **Notices** column next to each video. (Older guides call this the "Restrictions" column. The current YouTube Help page uses "Notices". TODO: check the Studio wording on the day of publishing.)
4. Hover over a notice to see the **Impact summary**. It shows the cause and sometimes a button to take action.

YouTube Help lists these kinds of notices: copyright, terms and policies, age restriction, made for kids, and ad suitability. Each one limits something different. For example, an age restriction or an ad suitability notice is a real restriction, but it is not the same as hidden reach.

If a video has a notice, read it and follow its instructions. If no video has a notice, no restriction is shown for your content.

## Step 2: Check your feature access

Some features depend on what your channel has verified. In desktop YouTube Studio:

1. Click **Settings**.
2. Click **Channel**.
3. Click **Feature eligibility**.

YouTube Help describes standard, intermediate and advanced access. Standard features are available to a new channel as long as it has no active Community Guidelines strikes. Intermediate features need phone verification. Advanced features need more channel history or an identity check.

A feature you cannot use is not the same as lower reach. But it tells you whether your channel has a problem on record.

## Step 3: Rule out the normal causes of fewer impressions

YouTube's own help page "Decoding CTR & impressions in your Analytics" explains several normal reasons. In plain words:

- **A wider audience clicks less.** YouTube Help says that as a video reaches more people, its click-through rate (CTR) can fall. Its example is a video whose CTR drops from 9% to 3.5% while impressions grow from 10,000 to 100,000. That is still 90,000 more impressions.
- **Early CTR is often inflated.** Loyal viewers see a video first, and they click more. The number can drop when the video reaches new viewers.
- **Traffic sources behave differently.** Search usually has fewer impressions and a higher CTR. Homepage usually has more impressions and a lower CTR.
- **Good numbers do not guarantee more reach.** YouTube Help says a very niche topic can run out of interested viewers, and a popular topic faces strong competition.

So before you decide something is hidden, open **Analytics → Reach** in Studio and look at which traffic source changed. A drop in one source tells you a different story than a drop in all of them.

## Step 4: Compare recent videos with older ones

Pick your last five uploads and five older uploads of a similar kind. For each one, write down:

- Impressions
- Impressions click-through rate
- Average view duration
- Main traffic source

Then look for a pattern. If one video fell and the rest are normal, the problem is that video, not your channel. If every recent video has a weaker title and thumbnail, or a shorter average view duration, the problem is in the work, not a ban.

Do this over a period long enough to include several videos. One weak video is not a trend. (TODO: if the Growati team has its own data on how many videos or days are enough, add it here. We did not find an official YouTube number.)

## What not to do

- **Do not trust a "shadowban checker" on its own.** We could not verify how these tools work. A "negative" result from one is a warning, not proof.
- **Do not delete videos in a panic.** You lose the history you need to find the cause.
- **Do not change everything at once.** If you change titles, thumbnails and upload time together, you cannot tell which change helped.
- **Do not skip the notices.** A real restriction is the one case where acting on it is the fix.

## FAQ

### Is YouTube shadowbanning my channel?
Most likely not. We did not find a YouTube Help page that describes a shadowban. YouTube does document visible restrictions and normal causes of lower reach. Check the Notices column in Studio, then your traffic sources, before you assume anything is hidden.

### How do I check if my YouTube channel has restrictions?
Open YouTube Studio, select Content, and look at the Notices column for each video. Hover over a notice to see the impact summary. For feature access, go to Settings, then Channel, then Feature eligibility.

### Why did my YouTube impressions suddenly drop?
Common reasons: one traffic source shrank, recent thumbnails and titles get fewer clicks, or the video moved on to a wider audience. YouTube Help notes that CTR can fall as impressions rise. Compare your last five videos with five older ones.

### Can a Community Guidelines strike lower my reach?
YouTube Help says a channel needs to be free of active Community Guidelines strikes to get standard features. A strike appears in Studio. Whether it changes how often your videos are shown is something we could not confirm from YouTube Help. TODO: add the official wording if found.

### Do shadowban checker tools work?
We could not verify how they work, and we found no official support for them. Treat any result as a hint at most. Studio is the source that YouTube itself shows you.

### How long should I wait before deciding my channel has a problem?
