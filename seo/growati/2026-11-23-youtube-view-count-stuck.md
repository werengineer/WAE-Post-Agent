## In short

- A frozen public view count is usually a counting delay, not lost views.
- YouTube says it checks views to make sure they come from real people, and that this takes time.
- Check YouTube Analytics. YouTube calls it "the most timely and accurate source of views data."
- Do not delete and re-upload the video because of a frozen counter. It does not fix the count.

## What YouTube says about frozen view counts

YouTube's Help page on views says a count may "seem frozen" on some videos and channels. YouTube wants views to come from real people and not from computer programs. Its systems need time to decide which views are legitimate.

The Help page also says YouTube may temporarily slow, freeze or change counts. It may discard low-quality playbacks, for example the same video playing in several tabs or on several devices at once.

In a past incident, YouTube's own blog post "Update on frozen viewcounts" said that public counts were stuck on some videos, but "all your views are still being recorded, and are visible in YouTube Analytics." It said it expected the fix "within the next couple of days." (TODO: the blog post shows no date in the text we read; check the date before you quote it.)

## Why a count can look stuck

- **The video is new.** YouTube says new videos can take "the first few hours" before metrics show up.
- **Views are being checked.** YouTube says verified metrics can take "several days" to fully update.
- **Some playbacks are not counted.** Low-quality playbacks can be removed, so the number may pause or move less than you expect.
- **You are looking at Realtime.** YouTube says Realtime "only shows estimates of potential view activity," so it may not match the watch page.

You may also have read that views "freeze at 301." A 2012 Digital Trends article explained that YouTube then started checking views once a video passed 300. That article is old, and it does not say whether the system works the same way today. Treat it as history, not as a current rule.

## Where to see your real numbers

1. Open YouTube Studio.
2. Go to Analytics.
3. Look at the video's views and compare them with the public count.

Use Analytics over the watch page while the count looks stuck. Give new uploads a few hours, and give older videos a few days, before you decide something is wrong.

## What to do, and what not to do

**Do:**
- Wait, then check Analytics again.
- Note the date and the number you see on the watch page and in Analytics.
- If the gap stays for several days, report it through YouTube Help (TODO: confirm the current report path in YouTube Studio before publishing).

**Do not:**
- Re-upload the video to "reset" the count. You lose the video's history and start from zero.
- Buy views or use bots to push the number. YouTube is checking that views come from real people.
- Change the title and thumbnail in a panic. A frozen counter is not a sign that the packaging is wrong.

## When it is not a counting problem

If Analytics also shows few views, the count is not frozen. The video is just not being shown or clicked. Then look at:

- **Title and thumbnail.** Do people click when they see them?
- **Visibility.** Is the video public and not restricted?
- **The first 30 seconds.** Do viewers stay?

## FAQ

### Why is my YouTube view count stuck?
YouTube says a count may "seem frozen" while its systems check that views come from real people. This can take hours or days. Your views are still recorded and show in YouTube Analytics.

### How long does a frozen view count last?
YouTube says new videos can take "the first few hours" to show metrics and that verified metrics can take "several days." It gives no fixed time. If the gap lasts longer than that, report it to YouTube.

### Does a frozen view count mean I lost views?
No. In its own post on frozen counts, YouTube said all views were still being recorded and visible in YouTube Analytics. The public number is just late.

### Why does my video show 0 views?
Brand new videos can take a few hours to show metrics. If Analytics also shows no views after a few days, check that the video is public and not restricted, then look at the title and thumbnail.

### Why is YouTube Realtime different from the watch page?
YouTube says Realtime only shows estimates of potential view activity. The watch page shows counts after YouTube has checked them, so the two numbers can differ.

### Should I delete and re-upload a video with a stuck count?
No. Re-uploading does not fix a frozen counter. It creates a new video and you start again from zero.
