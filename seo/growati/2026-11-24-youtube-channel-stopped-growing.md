# Why did my YouTube channel stop growing?

## In short

A channel that stops growing has usually stalled at one of three points: YouTube shows the videos to fewer people (impressions), fewer of those people click (click-through rate), or the people who click leave early (retention). Check them in that order in YouTube Studio. The first one that dropped tells you what to fix. Subscriber count and total views are poor guides, because they only tell you that something changed, not where.

## Step 0: Is there enough data to judge?

Pick a window before you compare anything. Compare the last 28 days with the 28 days before, and, if the channel is old enough, with the same weeks a year ago. A dip that repeats every year is seasonal, not a problem with your videos.

Very new videos also have thin numbers. If a video is only a day or two old, wait before you draw a conclusion. YouTube's own help page says retention data takes 1 to 2 days to process.

## Step 1: Are impressions down?

An impression is a time YouTube showed your thumbnail to a viewer. In YouTube Studio, open Analytics, then Reach, and compare impressions across your two windows.

- **Impressions fell on many videos at once.** Something changed around the channel: upload gaps, a new topic, or a seasonal drop.
- **Impressions fell on one video.** Look at that video alone. The fix is usually in its packaging or topic.
- **Impressions are steady.** Go to Step 2. Reach is not your problem.

## Step 2: Is click-through rate down?

Click-through rate (CTR) is the share of impressions that turned into a view. If impressions are steady but CTR fell, viewers see your videos and pass. Recent titles and thumbnails are the first thing to compare. Do the last few look alike? Does the title promise something clear?

Read CTR with care. YouTube Help says differences in CTR "might be due to traffic sources, rather than the title or thumbnail." For example, impressions on the Home page tend to lower CTR, while impressions from your channel page tend to give a higher one. If your mix of traffic changed, CTR can move without any change to your thumbnails. Check Step 4 before you redesign anything.

## Step 3: Do viewers leave early?

If impressions and CTR look fine, open a video's Audience retention report. YouTube's "Key moments" view shows an Intro figure: the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds. According to YouTube Help, key moments appear only for videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views.

- **A big drop in the first 30 seconds** usually means the title or thumbnail promised something the opening did not deliver, or the opening is slow.
- **Dips later in the video** mark the sections where viewers skipped ahead or left. Watch those parts.
- **A gradual decline** is normal.

Changing the thumbnail will not fix a retention problem. It is a different step.

## Step 4: Did the traffic source change?

In Analytics, open Reach and look at traffic sources. Compare the two windows. If most of your growth came from one source (for example, Search or Suggested videos) and that source shrank, the channel looks flat even though your newest videos perform as before. Also split viewers into new and returning, and subscribers and non-subscribers. A channel can look flat when it keeps its regular viewers but stops reaching new ones.

## What to do this week

1. Write down the dates of your two windows.
2. Record impressions, CTR and average view duration for each.
3. Find the first metric that dropped (Step 1, then 2, then 3).
4. Change one thing on one video, such as the title or the first 30 seconds of the next upload.
5. Wait for enough data before you judge it. Do not change five things at once, or you will not know what worked.

## FAQ

### Why did my YouTube channel suddenly stop growing?
Usually impressions, click-through rate or retention dropped on your recent videos. Compare the last 28 days with the 28 days before in YouTube Studio, then check impressions first, CTR second, and retention third. The first one that fell is where to start.

### Which YouTube metric should I check first?
Start with impressions in the Reach tab. If YouTube shows your videos to fewer people, no title change will help much. If impressions are steady, check CTR, then retention.

### Is a drop in subscribers the same as a drop in growth?
No. Subscriber count is a result, not a cause. Impressions, CTR and retention show where viewers are lost, so check them before you act on a slow subscriber count.

### Can seasonality make my channel look like it stopped growing?
Yes. Compare the same weeks in earlier years if your channel is old enough. A dip that repeats every year is a seasonal pattern, not a flaw in your videos. TODO: add an example only if the team has real channel data.

### Can a thumbnail change fix low retention?
Usually not. Retention measures what happens after the click. If viewers leave in the first 30 seconds, look at whether the opening delivers what the title and thumbnail promised.
