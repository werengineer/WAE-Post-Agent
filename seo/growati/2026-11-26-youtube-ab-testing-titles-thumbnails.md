# Does YouTube have built-in A/B testing for titles and thumbnails?

*Author: TODO (name of the Growati author). Published: TODO. Last checked against YouTube Help: 2026-11-26.*

## The short answer

Yes. YouTube Studio has a built-in feature, labelled **A/B Testing**, that lets you test up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails on one video. It was earlier called "Test & Compare", and many people still search for that name. When the test ends, YouTube shows the option with the highest **watch time share** to all viewers. It does not pick by click-through rate. It is a desktop-only feature in YouTube Studio, and Shorts cannot be tested.

## Who can run a test, and on which videos

According to YouTube Help ("A/B test titles & thumbnails"):

- You use it in **YouTube Studio on desktop**.
- You must have **advanced features** enabled.
- These videos **can** be tested: regular long-form videos and Live Archives. Premieres become eligible once the Premiere ends and the video converts to long-form.
- These videos **cannot** be tested: Shorts, scheduled Lives, active Premieres, videos made for kids, videos marked mature or age-restricted, and private videos.
- Uploads of up to three minutes that are square or vertical are treated as Shorts. A video that becomes a Short loses access to its existing tests.

## How to set up an A/B test in YouTube Studio

1. Sign in to YouTube Studio on a computer and pick the video. For a new video, upload it with Create. For an existing one, open it from Content.
2. Click **A/B Testing** in the Title box or under Thumbnail.
3. Choose **Title only**, **Thumbnail only**, or **Title and thumbnail**.
4. Upload up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails.
5. Click **Done**.

To check on a running test, open Analytics, go to the **Reach** tab, and look for the test panel. Click **Manage test** to review or stop it. Results also show on the video's Details page.

## How YouTube picks the winner

YouTube says the option with the highest **watch time share** is shown to all viewers. In plain words: it compares how much total viewing time each version earned, not just how many people clicked. A thumbnail that gets clicks but loses viewers quickly can lose to one that gets fewer clicks and holds people longer.

This matters if you are used to judging a thumbnail by CTR alone. In a YouTube test, a lower CTR can still win.

A small share of viewers may see only the default title and thumbnail. YouTube excludes their data from the results.

## What Winner, Performed Same and Inconclusive mean

YouTube Help lists three outcomes:

| Result | Meaning | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Winner** | One option clearly did better than the others. | That option is shown to all viewers. |
| **Performed Same** | The options did about equally well. | You can choose the one you prefer. The first one you uploaded is the default. |
| **Inconclusive** | YouTube found no strong difference. | The first title and thumbnail you uploaded become the default. You can change it afterwards. |

If your versions are very similar, YouTube says the test may run longer or end inconclusive. Make the versions clearly different.

## How long a test takes

YouTube says tests should finish **within two weeks**. Some take a few days. It depends on how many impressions the video gets and how recently it was published. YouTube does not publish a fixed number of impressions needed (TODO: confirm if YouTube Help adds one).

## Mistakes that stop or spoil a test

- **Editing the title or thumbnail during the test.** The test stops automatically and you must restart it.
- **Testing a video that is a Short, kids, private or age-restricted.** These are not eligible.
- **Uploading thumbnails smaller than 1280 x 720.** YouTube says that if any test thumbnail is below 720p, all test thumbnails are shown at 480p.
- **Testing near-identical versions.** You will likely get "Performed Same" or "Inconclusive".
- **Testing many things at once.** If you change the title and the thumbnail together, you will know which pair won but not which part made the difference. Use "Title only" or "Thumbnail only" when you want a clear answer.

YouTube also suggests testing older videos first to limit the effect on your overall channel views.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does YouTube have built-in A/B testing?
Yes. In YouTube Studio on desktop, click **A/B Testing** in the Title box or under Thumbnail. You can test up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails on one video. The feature was earlier called "Test & Compare".

### Is it free?
YouTube Help does not mention any charge for the feature. TODO: confirm in a live Studio account before stating "free" as a fact in the published page, or keep this wording.

### How does YouTube decide the winner?
By watch time share. The option that earns the highest share of watch time is shown to all viewers. It is not chosen by click-through rate alone.

### How long does a YouTube A/B test take?
YouTube says tests should finish within two weeks. They can take a few days, depending on impressions and how new the video is.

### Can I A/B test YouTube Shorts?
No. Shorts cannot be tested. Scheduled Lives, active Premieres, kids videos, private videos and age-restricted videos are also not eligible.

### What if the result is "Performed Same" or "Inconclusive"?
"Performed Same" means the options did about equally well, so you can choose the one you like. With "Inconclusive", YouTube found no strong difference, and the first title and thumbnail you uploaded become the default. You can change it by hand afterwards.

### What happens if I change the title or thumbnail during a test?
The test stops automatically. You need to restart it.
