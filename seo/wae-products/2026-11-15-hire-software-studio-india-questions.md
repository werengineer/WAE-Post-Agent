## Body

### Short answer
Before you hire a software studio in India, ask ten things: who owns the code, who does the daily work, how you are charged, whether you can pause or cancel, when you get a first plan, whether you can see live work, whether you can speak to past clients, what support follows launch, which technology they use, and whether you can start with a small test. A good studio answers each one in writing and without hesitation.

### 1. Do I own all the code and IP?
Ask this first. You should be able to take the code to another team the next day if you choose. Ask for the ownership term in the contract, not only on the sales call. If the studio hesitates, treat it as a warning.

*How WAE Projects answers:* the WAE Projects site states that the client owns all code and IP. Put this in your contract as well.

### 2. Who will work on my project every day?
Ask for names or roles, how senior they are, and whether they are dedicated to you or shared with other clients. A "team" on the proposal can mean different people in practice.

*How WAE Projects answers:* the site lists a dedicated senior developer on its Standard plan and a dedicated squad (developers, UI, QA) on its Premium plan.

### 3. How is the work priced?
Studios usually offer fixed price, hourly, or monthly dedicated-team pricing. Each suits a different project. Fixed price works when scope is clear. Monthly plans suit products that change as you learn.

*How WAE Projects answers:* it sells monthly plans in Indian rupees. ### 4. Can I pause or cancel?
Lock-in is a common source of regret. Ask about notice periods, penalties, and what you get if you stop midway.

*How WAE Projects answers:* the site says all plans allow pausing or cancelling without penalty.

### 5. When will I see a first plan?
A studio that understands your idea can describe a roadmap and an estimate quickly. Ask how long it takes and whether it costs anything.

*How WAE Projects answers:* the site offers a free MVP roadmap and technical cost breakdown within 24 hours of an inquiry.

Open the live products. Click through them on your phone. Check that they load and work.

A reliable studio can connect you with current or past clients. Ask for two, and ask them about delays and communication, not only the final result.

Ask who fixes bugs, who handles hosting, and what response time you can expect.

You should understand the choice well enough to hire someone else later. Common, widely supported tools make that easier.
