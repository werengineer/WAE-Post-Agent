# How to Start a Sharing Club in Your Community

A sharing club is a group of people who lend and borrow things, or offer skills, instead of each person buying everything alone. A drill used twice a year, a camping tent, a stack of books: these sit unused in many homes. A sharing club puts them to work. This guide gives six plain steps to start one.

## What is a sharing club?

A sharing club is a group that agrees to share items, space or skills. Members borrow what they need and lend what they do not use often. Some clubs work like a "library of things" with a shared collection. Others are simply a list of what each member is happy to lend. Clubs can be as small as one street, a college hostel or an office floor.

## Step 1: Find a few people and a shared goal

Do not start alone. Talk to a few friends or neighbours first and agree on one goal, such as "save money on tools" or "cut waste". Guides on starting a library of things, such as those from Shareable, also advise building a small core team before anything else.

## Step 2: Check what people need

Ask before you build. A short question to your group works: "What do you buy rarely but need sometimes?" The answers tell you which items will be borrowed most. Guides on community libraries describe this as a needs check, and it saves you from collecting things nobody wants.

## Step 3: Choose what to share

Start with a short list. Good first items are easy to carry, easy to check and not too costly:
- Hand tools and small appliances
- Books and board games
- Sports and camping gear
- Skills, such as a one-hour help session

Avoid anything dangerous or very expensive until you have a track record.

## Step 4: Write simple rules

Rules prevent arguments. Keep them short and put them in writing:
1. Who can join?
2. How long can an item be kept?
3. What happens if something is late, lost or broken?
4. Is there a fee or is it free? (Decide this early; it shapes how you cover costs.)
5. Who is the contact person?

If you plan a formal organisation or collect money, ask a local legal adviser. This guide is not legal advice.

## Step 5: Pick a place and a way to keep track

Items need a home, such as a member's garage, a college room or a community space. You also need a record of who has what. A shared spreadsheet is enough to begin. ## Step 6: Launch small and improve

Start with one group and a small list for a month. Then ask members what worked and what did not. Add items and members slowly. Tell people about the club through a message group, a notice board or word of mouth.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Starting with too many items
- Having no written rules
- Having no one in charge of the list
- Not saying what happens when something breaks

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a sharing club?
A sharing club is a group of people who lend and borrow items or skills among themselves, so each person owns and buys less.

### How many people do I need to start a sharing club?
There is no fixed number. A few friends or neighbours are enough to begin. Grow once the basic rules work.

### What items are best to share first?
Choose items that are easy to carry and not too costly, such as hand tools, books, games and camping gear.

### Do I need to charge a fee?
No rule says you must. Some clubs are free and some charge a small membership. Decide early and write it in your rules.

### What if an item is lost or broken?
Agree on this before anyone borrows. Common choices are to repair it together, replace it, or accept normal wear. Write the choice in your rules.

### Do I need an app to run a sharing club?
No. A spreadsheet or message group works for a small club. An app becomes useful when many members and items need tracking.

### Is this legal advice?
No. If you form a company, charity or co-op, or handle money, speak to a local legal adviser.
