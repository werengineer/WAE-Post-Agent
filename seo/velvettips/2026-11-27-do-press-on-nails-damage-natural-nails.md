# Do press-on nails damage your natural nails?

## The short answer

Usually not, if you apply and remove them with care. Press-ons do not need filing or drilling of your natural nail, which is why many people find them gentler than methods that do. The damage people see (thin, peeling or brittle nails) most often comes from pulling a set off, wearing sets back to back, or using the wrong size.

Doctors do list real risks, so it is worth knowing them. A Cleveland Clinic dermatologist, Taryn Murray, MD, names three: infection (press-ons can trap moisture, which fungus and bacteria like), allergic reaction to the nail or the glue, and nail damage such as brittleness and breakage from applying and removing fake nails.

## What actually causes damage

- **Pulling or prying the set off.** Dermatologist Hannah Kopelman told NBC Select that people get impatient and rip or peel nails off, which often takes a layer of the natural nail with it.
- **Never giving nails a break.** Wearing sets back to back can lead to brittleness, peeling and cuticle infections, according to the same article.
- **Gaps and poor fit.** A gap between your nail and the press-on can trap water and dirt. A set that is too small can press on the nail. Measure before you buy: see our [size guide](https://velvettips.com/size-guide).
- **Too much glue or too much buffing.** Use a small amount of glue and only smooth the surface lightly.
- **Long nails.** Dr. Murray says longer nails are more likely to cause separation between the natural nail and the nail bed. Shorter shapes are the safer choice if you are worried.

## How to apply press-ons without harming your nails

1. Wash and dry your hands. Push back your cuticles gently with the pusher from your kit.
2. Lightly buff the surface of each nail with the file or buffer in your kit. Do not over-buff.
3. Wipe each nail with the alcohol prep pad and let it dry.
4. Match each press-on to a nail. Pick the size that covers the full nail without touching the skin.
5. Choose your adhesive. Adhesive tabs suit short wear. Nail glue suits longer wear. If you use glue, use a thin layer.
6. Press firmly for several seconds, with no gaps at the edges.

See also: [how to apply press-on nails](https://velvettips.com/how-to-apply).

## How to remove press-ons safely

1. Soak your hands in warm soapy water to loosen the adhesive.
2. Lift each nail gently from the sides. Never pull it straight off.
3. If a nail will not lift, soak again. Do not force it.
4. Remove leftover glue or tab residue from your nails, then moisturise your cuticles.

Some experts quoted by NBC Select suggest an acetone soak or a glue remover for glued-on sets. Acetone can ruin a set you want to wear again, so use warm soapy water if you plan to reuse it. ## How long should you rest between sets?

Experts quoted by NBC Select say press-ons typically last about two weeks and suggest giving your natural nails a break of about one to two weeks before the next set. Dr. Murray goes further and suggests wearing press-ons for special occasions. Velvet Tips guidance: rest your nails between sets and keep cuticles moisturised. ## Press-ons vs salon nails: which is gentler?

Several sources describe press-ons as gentler than acrylics or gels, which involve filing the natural nail and stronger chemicals. Our own comparison says press-ons applied and removed carefully are gentler than methods that involve filing, drilling or soaking in strong removers. Care is the key factor either way. Read: [press-on vs salon nails](https://velvettips.com/blog/press-on-vs-salon-nails).

## When to stop and see a doctor

Take the set off and see a dermatologist if you notice swelling, redness, tenderness around the nail, discoloration, or any change in how your nails look or grow. Stop using an adhesive if your skin reacts to it. Avoid wearing press-ons over raw or broken skin.

This guide is general information, not medical advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do press-on nails ruin your natural nails?
No, not when they are applied and removed gently. Pulling a set off is the main cause of damage. Soak, lift from the sides, and rest your nails between sets.

### Are press-ons or acrylics worse for your nails?
Acrylics and gels need the natural nail to be filed and use stronger chemicals, so several sources call press-ons the gentler option. Careless removal can harm your nails with either.

### Can press-on nails cause a fungal infection?
They can if moisture gets trapped under the nail. Dermatologist Taryn Murray, MD, of Cleveland Clinic warns of this. Avoid gaps, keep nails short, dry your hands well, and remove the set if you see swelling, redness or discoloration.

### How often can I wear press-on nails?
You can wear them for events or regularly with breaks. Experts suggest resting your natural nails for about one to two weeks after a set that has lasted around two weeks.

### How do I remove press-ons without damaging my nails?
Soak your hands in warm soapy water, then lift each nail gently from the sides. Never pull or peel.

### Is nail glue or a tab better for my nails?
Tabs are easier to remove and suit short wear. Glue lasts longer but needs more careful removal. Use only a thin layer of glue.

### Can I be allergic to press-on nails?
Yes. A reaction can be to the nail or the glue and can appear at any time. Stop use and speak to a doctor if your skin reacts.
