# How to Explain Your Final Year Project in a Viva

## The short answer

In a project viva you must be able to say five things clearly: what problem you solved and why, how you built it and why you chose that method, what you personally did, how you tested it, and what its limits are. Prepare a one-minute summary and one honest answer for each of those five points. Examiners want to hear you talk about your own work. They are not trying to trick you.

## What the viva is really checking

Most students revise theory as if it were a written exam. Theory can come up as a warm-up. The panel mostly wants to know whether the work is yours and whether you understand it.

- **Do you understand it?** Can you explain a diagram or a block of code without reading it out?
- **Is it yours?** Can you say which parts you did?
- **Did you test it?** Can you show how you know it works?
- **Are you honest about it?** Can you say what does not work yet?

## The questions examiners ask, and how to answer

The questions below are common ones. They are drawn from university handbooks and student guides (see the report for sources). No fixed list exists, so adapt them to your own project.

### Problem and motivation

- *What is the main goal of your project?* Answer in two sentences: who has the problem, and what your project does about it.
- *Why did you choose this topic?* Give a true reason. A real interest or a real problem you saw is better than "it is trending".

### Method and design choices

- *Why did you choose this method, tool, or technology?* Name one or two other options you considered and say why you did not use them.
- *Can you explain this diagram?* Practise walking through every diagram in your report from left to right, in plain words.
- *What would the result be with a different approach?* If you do not know, say what you expect and why.

### Your own contribution (for team projects)

- *Which part did you build?* Name the parts, and name who did what.
- *Did you write new code, algorithms or designs, or change existing ones?* Say exactly what is new and what you reused. Say where the reused material came from.

Every team member should be ready to answer about the hardest and the most time-consuming parts, not only their own.

### Testing and results

- *How do you know it works?* Describe your tests, the results, and one bug you found and fixed.
- *Which part was the hardest?* Pick a real problem and tell the short story: what failed, what you tried, what worked.

### Limits and future work

- *What are the weak points?* Name two real limits. Honest answers sound confident.
- *If you started again, what would you change?* Pick one concrete change.
- *How could this project be taken further?* Give one or two next steps.

## A 7-day preparation plan

1. **Day 1:** Re-read your whole report. Mark anything you cannot explain.
2. **Day 2:** Write the one-minute summary: problem, method, your part, result, limit.
3. **Day 3:** Practise explaining every diagram and the main flow of your code or circuit out loud.
4. **Day 4:** Write down the five questions you fear most. Write an honest answer for each.
5. **Day 5:** Revise the basic theory behind your method. Keep it short and clear.
6. **Day 6:** Do a mock viva with a friend, senior, or mentor. Ask them to interrupt you.
7. **Day 7:** Check that your demo runs. Keep a backup (screenshots or a recording). Rest.

## Mistakes that cost marks

- Reading from slides or from the report.
- Saying "it is in the code" instead of explaining it.
- Claiming work that is not yours. Panels often ask follow-up questions to check.
- Hiding the limits of the project.
- Blaming a teammate.
- Having a demo that has never been tested on another machine.

## FAQ

### What questions are asked in a final year project viva?
Examiners usually ask about your goal and why you chose the topic, your method and design choices, your own contribution, how you tested the work, and its limits and future scope. Some also ask basic theory. Questions differ by college and branch.

### How do I prepare for a final year project viva?
Read your full report, write a one-minute summary, practise explaining every diagram, list the questions you fear most, and do a mock viva. Start at least a week early, and test your demo on another machine.

### How do I answer "What is your contribution to the project?"
Say clearly which parts you designed, built and tested. Separate your work from reused code, libraries or designs, and name the source of the reused parts. For a team project, also say how your part connects to the others.

### How long should my project presentation be?
It depends on your college. Ask your guide or check the project handbook. TODO: the team may add typical time ranges only if a source is found.

### What if I do not know the answer to a question?
Say so honestly, then explain how you would find the answer or what you expect and why. Do not guess confidently. Examiners value clear thinking more than a made-up answer.

### Is a mock viva worth doing?
Yes. A mock viva with a friend, senior or mentor shows the weak points in your explanation while there is still time to fix them.
